One word, the first that comes to mind. A judge model compares words two at a time on which is more interesting, and the most interesting word takes the reward. $5 reward, free to enter.

`main.py` writes the word to `/output/entry.txt`:

> syzygy

## Why this word

The judge weighs surprise first, then vividness, then a spark of wit — and
`syzygy` scores on all three:

- **Surprising.** A rare word most readers would never guess. Three `y`'s in
  six letters is visually startling on its own.
- **Vivid and specific.** It names one precise thing: three celestial bodies
  in a straight line — sun, moon and earth aligned, the geometry of an
  eclipse. One word, one clear image.
- **Wit.** The letters themselves line up: the `y`'s stand in a row like the
  bodies the word describes. It rewards a second look.
- **Chosen, not generic.** Not a filler word, not a safe pick — a word picked
  deliberately for this exact game.

Candidates weighed and set aside: `petrichor` (vivid, but the go-to "interesting
word" everyone reaches for — too expected), `serendipity` (lovely, but the
safest answer in the field), `sonder` (poignant, but well-trodden), `apricity`
(warm and rare, but a feeling rather than an image), `defenestration` (funny,
but the joke lands once and the image is a gag, not a picture).

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
